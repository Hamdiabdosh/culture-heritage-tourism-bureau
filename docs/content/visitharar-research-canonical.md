## B. City facts (canonical)



### B1. UNESCO status and inscription year

- **Name:** Harar Jugol, the Fortified Historic Town  
- **Status:** UNESCO World Heritage Site  
- **Inscribed:** 16 July 2006 (30th Session of the World Heritage Committee)  
- **Criteria:** (ii), (iii), (iv), and (v)  
- **Core zone:** 48 hectares (the entire walled historic town)  
- **Coordinates (nomination):** N 09°19′00″ E 42°07′00″  
- **UNESCO framing:** Interchange of Islamic and African (esp. southern Ethiopian) traditions; exceptional testimony to living cultural traditions; outstanding architectural/urban ensemble; traditional settlement and *afocha* relationship with the environment

**Sources:** Nomination dossier 1189rev; [UNESCO WHC 1189](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1189/)

---



### B2. Why Harar matters

- **Faith:** UNESCO criterion (iii) states Harar Jugol is considered **“the fourth holy city” of Islam**, founded by a holy missionary from the Arabian Peninsula. The fortified sacred Muslim city numbers **82 mosques** (three from the 10th century) and **102 shrines** (WHC brief; nomination text also notes historically ~99 mosques of which 82 remain, and ~103 shrines — prefer **82 / 102** for public copy). Continuous Islamic learning and pilgrimage.  
- **Trade:** From the late 16th to the 19th century, a noted centre of trade and Islamic learning linking coast and Ethiopian interior. Capital of the Harari Kingdom 1520–1568; independent emirate from the 17th century; Egyptian occupation ~10 years; incorporated into Ethiopia **1887**.  
- **Living culture:** Functioning historic community. Nomination highlights *afocha* (neighbourhood associations for weddings/funerals and social rules), Harari language, traditional townhouses (interiors = “most spectacular part” of the heritage), and handicrafts: weaving, basket-making, book-binding. Three house types: traditional Harari, Indian merchant (after 1887), and hybrid.

**Sources:** Nomination OUV / description; WHC brief description

---



### B3. “City of Peace” / hospitality meaning

Harar’s sacred identity is also expressed as **Madīnat al-Awliyā’** (“City of Saints”) in Arabic tradition (secondary/local usage — keep). UNESCO’s own inscription text already frames the city as a Muslim centre enclosed within a wider Christian region whose cultural specificity and community structure (*afocha*) remain alive.

Locally and regionally it is also presented as a **“City of Peace”** (sometimes “City of Peace and Tolerance”), reflecting intercommunal coexistence and hospitality. UNESCO **Cities for Peace** recognition in the early 2000s remains **year UNVERIFIED** in our packs (2002 / 2003 / 2002–2003) — not stated in the 2004 nomination dossier.

**Visitor-copy rules:**

- Frame as local ideal + recognised coexistence — not a generic marketing slogan.  
- Do **not** claim the city never knew conflict, or that every visit is automatically peaceful.  
- Hospitality is lived practice (homes, coffee, *afocha*), not a tourist performance.

### B5. Best seasons / climate note for travelers

Harar sits on a highland plateau (eastern Ethiopia), milder than surrounding deserts/savannah and nearby lowlands such as Dire Dawa. Days typically warm (often mid-20s °C); mornings and evenings cooler.


| Period               | Guidance                                                 |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Best for walking** | Roughly **October–March** (especially November–February) |
| **Wetter**           | More rain **April–May** and parts of **July–September**  |
| **Pack**             | Walking shoes, sun protection, light layer               |


General traveler guidance only — not from the nomination dossier. Check current weather before travel.

### B6. Safety & respect basics

- **Dress:** Conservative clothing inside Jugol. Cover shoulders and knees; scarf useful for women near mosques/shrines.  
- **Photography:** Ask before photographing people; never during prayer without permission; interiors often restricted.  
- **Faith sites:** Assume non-Muslim interior access restricted unless a guide confirms. Shoes off where others have. Quiet during prayer. Avoid Friday midday congestion.  
- **Orientation / after dark:** Unlit maze — local guide strongly recommended for first visits and evenings.  
- **General:** Secure valuables in markets; check official travel advisories.

---



### B8. Getting around

- **Inside Jugol:** **Foot only** — dense maze; no ordinary vehicle circulation in the historic core.  
- **Outside walls:** Bajaj for short trips; minibuses/taxis for longer local hops (e.g. hyena sites).  
- **Entry logic:** Historic gates open to streets leading toward the centre; **Harar Gate** (west) connects via the modern “straight street” to **Faras Magala**; commercial spine continues to **Gidir Magala** via **Makina Girgir**.
