## B. City facts (Harar Jugol)

### B1. UNESCO status and inscription year

- **Status:** UNESCO World Heritage Site — *Harar Jugol, the Fortified Historic Town*
- **Inscribed:** 2006
- **Criteria:** (ii), (iii), (iv), (v)
- **Property size:** 48 hectares (historic walled town)
- **Significance (UNESCO framing):** Interaction of African and Islamic traditions; historic urban form; traditional Harari houses; religious heritage; living cultural traditions

**Sources:** [UNESCO WHC — Harar Jugol (1189)](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1189)

### B2. Why Harar matters

| Theme | Fact line |
|---|---|
| Faith | Historic centre of Islamic culture and learning in the Horn of Africa; UNESCO describes a sacred Muslim city with **82 mosques** and **102 shrines**, including three mosques dating to the 10th century |
| Trade | Important trading centre from late 16th through 19th century, connecting coast and interior highlands |
| Living culture | Functioning community: Harari language, handicrafts, houses, religious practice, and social systems remain part of everyday life |

**Sources:** [UNESCO WHC — Harar Jugol](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1189)

### B3. “City of Peace” / hospitality meaning

- Harari Regional Government presents Harar as a **“City of Peace and Tolerance”**, with peace, tolerance, and hospitality among regional values.
- **Visitor-copy rule:** Do **not** claim Harar never experienced conflict, or that every visit is automatically peaceful.
- **Accurate framing:** Peace, tolerance, and hospitality are important parts of how Harar and its institutions present cultural identity — alongside history as a religious, commercial, and multicultural centre.

**Sources:** [Harari Regional Government — Cultural Heritage and Tourism Bureau](https://www.harari.gov.et/en/office/cultural-heritage-and-tourism-bureau--1)

### B4. Rough scale visitors notice

| Element | Best visitor figure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Historic walls | 13th–16th centuries (broad UNESCO dating) | Do not force a single construction year |
| Historic gates | **5 original historic gates** | Plus later **Harar Gate** (newer); do not call “six historic gates” |
| Mosques | **82** | Prefer UNESCO over unsourced “99” |
| Shrines | **102** | UNESCO |
| Urban character | Narrow alleys, traditional houses, mosques/shrines, markets | Living town, not open-air museum |

**Sources:** [UNESCO WHC](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1189); [UNESCO document 151989](https://whc.unesco.org/document/151989)

### B5. Best seasons / climate note

- Highland / relatively moderate climate.
- Reported averages (environmental assessment): ~**12°C** (Dec) to ~**27.1°C** (Mar); avg max ~**25.5°C**, min ~**13.4°C**.
- Wetter periods often around **April–May** and **July–August**.
- **Practical sightseeing:** October–March generally drier / easier walking — **GENERAL GUIDANCE**, not an official tourism season.
- Pack: walking shoes, sun protection, light layer for cool mornings/evenings.

**Sources:** Ethiopian Ministry of Water and Energy environmental assessment (Harar WWTP/FSTP ESIA PDF referenced in research)

### B6. Safety & respect basics

- Jugol is a **living Muslim historic town**.
- Dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees near religious sites).
- Ask before photographing people — especially residents, women, worshippers.
- Do not photograph during prayer without permission.
- Follow caretaker instructions at mosques/shrines; do not assume all buildings are open to visitors.
- Remove shoes when required.
- Secure valuables in markets.
- Check current official travel advice before travel (conditions can change).

### B7. How to arrive

| Mode | Summary | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Air | Fly to **Dire Dawa (DIR)**, then road to Harar (~51–55 km; ~55–60 min by car, traffic-dependent) | Practical default |
| Road Dire Dawa ↔ Harar | ~1 hour; minibuses operate | OK |
| Road Addis Ababa | Bus ~11 hours (variable) | Approximate |
| Train | Addis–Djibouti railway to **Dire Dawa** (~11 hours subject to timetable), then road to Harar | OK |
| Direct train to Harar | **UNKNOWN / UNVERIFIED** | Do not claim |

### B8. Getting around

- **Inside Jugol:** Walk. Pedestrian historic core; maze of alleyways.
- **Outside walls:** Bajaj / three-wheelers common for modern Harar and transfers.
- **Do not expect** normal car access through narrow Jugol lanes.
- Expectation: drop near a suitable gate → explore on foot.

## E. Stories (draft set of 5)

### E1. The Gē Gār: Inside a Traditional Harari House

- **Category:** Architecture  
- **Summary:** The gē gār is a defining feature of Jugol — African and Islamic traditions meeting in living house architecture.  
- **Key facts to keep:** UNESCO common house type; distinctive interiors; living-town framing; host permission for visits.  
- **Related:** Traditional Harari House; Evening Jugol Cultural Walk; Basket Weaving; Coffee Ceremony  

### E2. The Hyenas of Harar: A Nighttime Tradition

- **Category:** Living culture  
- **Summary:** Long relationship between city and spotted hyenas; organized feeding often dated from the 1960s in secondary sources.  
- **Key facts to keep:** Wild animals; observe, don’t interfere; coexistence framing over spectacle.  
- **Confidence:** Secondary/Wikipedia-backed timeline — flag for Commission review before hard claims.  
- **Related:** Hyena Feeding; Evening Walk  

### E3. Coffee, Markets and the Roads That Made Harar

- **Category:** Coffee  
- **Summary:** Coffee sits inside hospitality + trade history; markets (Feres Magala, Gidir Magala) essential to the town’s survival.  
- **Related:** Coffee Ceremony; Market Walk; Feres Magala; Gē Gār  

### E4. Five Gates, One Walled City

- **Category:** Architecture  
- **Summary:** Walls (broadly 13th–16th c.), five historic gates + later Harar Gate; wall as organizer of movement and neighbourhoods.  
- **Related:** All five gate attractions; Evening Walk  

### E5. Harar: Faith, Neighbours and the Idea of a City of Peace

- **Category:** Faith & peace  
- **Summary:** 82 mosques / 102 shrines; interchange of Islamic and African traditions; “City of Peace” as identity, not conflict-free myth.
